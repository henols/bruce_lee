<source_findings>
Everything below was extracted from the Lemon64 transcription during investigation on
2026-08-03. It is reproduced here because the executor will not otherwise have it, and because
Task 3 logs it verbatim. Cross-check each item against the downloaded `.txt` as you go — that
cross-check is the verification.

**Provenance chain, stated in the page's own footer, verbatim:** *"Converted to HTML by Lemon64.
The Project 64 etext of the ~Bruce Lee manual~, converted to etext by anonymous, obtained from
the Asimov Apple ][ site, http://www.apple.asimov.net/site/documentation/games/brucelee/
BRUCEL10.TXT, April 1997, etext #200#"*

**CRITICAL — this is the Apple II manual, not the C64 one.** Its hardware section requires an
"Apple II(R) series computer" and an "Apple compatible disk drive", and instructs the reader to
"Insert the BRUCE LEE(TM) diskette". Game-design content (scoring, damage, hazards, moves) is
shared across ports; platform specifics (loading, controls hardware, memory) are not.

**Scoring table, exact values:**

| Action | Points |
|---|---|
| Lantern | 125 |
| Chopping ninja or Yamo | 100 |
| Kicking ninja or Yamo | 75 |
| Entering new room | 2000 |
| Knocking out ninja | 200 |
| Knocking out Yamo | 450 |
| Destroying wizard | 3000 |
| Landing on ninja or Yamo | 50 |

**Damage and lives:** "the Yamo can survive only three blows and the ninja can survive only two".
Five falls before game over in a one-player game.

**Named in-fiction hazards and objects:** electrical charges in the gaps between ledges; "PAN
lights streaming across the floor"; exploding "T'SUNG-LIN (bushes)"; the ninja's weapon is a
"BOKKEN" stick; the wizard streams fire balls from his eyes and is destroyed by a button.

**Movement verbs with their joystick inputs:** RUN; KICK (button while running); CHOP (button
while standing still); LEAP (up + left/right); JUMP; CLIMB (up a vine); DUCK (joystick down).

**Credits:** "Programming by Richard Mirsky / Concept by Ron J. Fortier and Kelly Day / Computer
graphics by Kelly Day / Documentation by Ingrid Holcomb". (C) 1984 Datasoft Inc. Licensed by Ziv
International. "Bruce Lee(TM) designates a trademark of Linda Lee."

**Finding (a) — the manual gives the ninja no proper name.** It says "the ninja" throughout,
lowercase and frequently plural ("the ninja brandishing their BOKKEN sticks"), against "the
Yamo" with a definite article. This is the negative result the `pin-canonical-character-names`
todo asks for — but it is **edition-scoped**: the Mastertronic and Datasoft C64 printings are
still unread.

**Finding (b) — the two-player mode described is turn-taking, not Yamo-vs-Bruce.** Verbatim: "In
a TWO-PLAYER GAME, you and another person take turns being Bruce, competing against the Yamo and
ninja. As soon as you (Bruce) take a fall, the other player takes a turn as Bruce."
`.planning/research/FEATURES.md:202` claims a mode where player 2 drives Yamo. Both may be true —
ports differed, and the C64 release is widely described as having a Yamo-controlling mode. **Do
not overwrite FEATURES.md.** Record the disagreement as an open question naming both sources, to
be settled by live execution.

**Finding (c) — credits attribution nuance.** `.claude/CLAUDE.md` frames the project as "Datasoft
/ Ron J. Fortier". This manual credits Fortier with *concept* and Richard Mirsky with
*programming* — for the Apple II edition. Fortier is conventionally credited as the Atari/C64
programmer. Note it; do **not** edit CLAUDE.md on the strength of an Apple II manual.
</source_findings>

<decisions>
**`poppler-utils` is NOT installed as part of this task. Deferred to the follow-up.** Three
reasons, in order of weight:

1. An apt install is **ephemeral container state, not an artifact**. It does not survive a
   devcontainer rebuild, so it is not something this task can "deliver" — whoever does the OCR
   work installs it then, in the session that uses it.
2. The urgency behind wanting OCR has evaporated. The reason to want the manual readable *today*
   was to get at its contents; the Lemon64 transcription already supplies them. What remains is
   the C64-vs-Apple-II diff, which is genuine work with its own scope, not a side effect of
   archiving.
3. This task's whole value is irreversibility-avoidance, and it should not acquire an unrelated
   network/privilege failure mode that could strand it before Task 1's commit lands.

The `register-the-primary-source-documents` todo keeps its step 2 open, annotated with this
reasoning so the decision is not re-litigated.
</decisions>
