## Criterion 1 — remaining live proofs (2026-08-05, plan 01.4-04)

### Proof A — `vice_result_continue`: NOT exercisable in normal use (honest negative)

Four tool shapes were escalated, each pushed to its documented maximum, specifically hunting for
the proxy's `OUTPUT_CHAR_CAP` (500,000 characters):

| # | Call | Parameters at max | Approx. response size | Chunked? |
|---|------|--------------------|------------------------|----------|
| 1 | `vice_memory_read` | `address:$0000, size:65535, encoding:"array", bank:"ram"` | ~330,000 chars (65535 × ~5 chars/entry) | No |
| 2 | `vice_display_screenshot` | `format:"BMP", return_base64:true` | ~140,000 chars (52,342-byte BMP, base64'd twice: `data_uri` + `base64`) | No |
| 3 | `vice_memory_search` | `start:$0000, end:$FFFF, pattern:[0], mask:[0], max_results:10000` | ~80,000 chars (10,000 × ~8-char `"$XXXX"` addresses) | No |
| 4 | `vice_memory_compare` | `mode:"ranges", range1_start:$0000, range1_end:$FFFE, range2_start:$0001, max_differences:10000` | very large (10,000 × ~48-char diff records), the closest of the four to the cap, still under it | No |

None of the four produced a continuation token or a chunk-count trailing marker. Every response
came back as a single self-contained JSON payload. This is a **structural** finding, not a lack of
effort: `vice_memory_read`'s `size` parameter is schema-capped at 65535 bytes, and at that hard cap
the `"array"` encoding's per-byte overhead (quote + 2 hex chars + comma ≈ 5 chars/byte) produces
~327,675 characters — arithmetically incapable of reaching 500,000 regardless of which address or
bank is read. `vice_memory_search` and `vice_memory_compare` are separately capped at 10,000
results/differences by their own schemas, and even the denser `memory_compare` record shape stayed
under the ceiling at that cap. `vice_display_screenshot`'s payload size is fixed by the C64's own
384×272 display geometry, not by anything the caller can inflate.

**Verdict: `vice_result_continue` is present and (per `01.4-01`/`01.4-02`'s prior evidence) callable
once its schema is loaded, but is honestly unreachable in normal use from this session's exposed
tool surface** — no combination of documented-maximum parameters across the four largest-output
tools available could trip the proxy's chunking threshold. This is the "cannot exceed the ceiling
even at maximum size" outcome the plan's own hazard note anticipated as an acceptable result,
recorded here rather than a manufactured pass. A future session with a *larger* single-call output
shape (not present in this tool surface as observed) could still trip it; this entry does not claim
the mechanism is unreachable in principle, only that it was unreachable from the tools this session
had.

### Proof B — `vice_recycle`: executed, deliberately, with full before/after evidence

Before-state (via `vice_diagnose`, zero emulator cost): verdict `live`, bracket 1 retired 35,145
cycles — the machine was healthy and running before the deliberate kill.

`vice_recycle` was called with a reason naming this as an intentional Phase 01.4 criterion-1
verification (not an incident). Its own response:

- Kill stage: `sigterm`. Epoch before: 1, epoch after: 1 (did **not** move within the recycle
  call's own timeout window — the epoch bump landed on a later observer, not synchronously inside
  the call).
- Readiness probe: not yet answering (`ECONNREFUSED`) at the moment `vice_recycle` returned.
- Snapshot accepted: `20260805160045723-port6603-epoch1`.
- Incident record written **before** anything was killed, per the skill's contract:
  `.planning/incidents/20260805160045723-port6603-epoch1.md` (in the **main workspace root**, not
  this worktree's own `.planning/incidents/` — consistent with `.claude/CLAUDE.md`'s documented
  path-resolution rule that host-side writes resolve against the workspace root regardless of the
  calling dispatch's own working tree). Contents verified: pre-kill cycle bracket (39,312 cycles),
  PC/register snapshot, checkpoint enumeration (none armed), and the stated reason, all present.

The very next forwarded call (`vice_ping`) hit the restarted-machine signal exactly as the plan
predicted:

> `vice-proxy: the broker connection was gone ... A replacement instance (port 6603) has already
> been acquired and adopted for this session -- the machine was REPLACED, the replacement is a
> FRESH emulator ... epoch drift detected ... epoch changed from 1 to 1, pid 3362603, spawned_at
> 2026-08-05T16:01:35.799Z.`

This is the same `"changed from 1 to 1"` reporting defect already filed in
`.planning/todos/pending/2026-08-05-epoch-drift-and-replacement-messages-name-impossible-values.md`
— a **third** independent confirmation that the epoch-number text is wrong while the underlying
replacement is genuine (proven here by the fresh `pid` and `spawned_at` fields the error itself
carries, not by the epoch number). A second `vice_ping` call afterward succeeded cleanly against
the new instance (`{"status":"ok","version":"3.10","machine":"C64SC","execution":"paused"}`), and a
follow-up `vice_diagnose` confirmed verdict `live` with a fresh cycle bracket (29,994 cycles),
proving the replacement instance is fully functional, not just reachable. The machine was resumed
once (`vice_execution_run`) to leave it in a running state afterward, per the plan's own closing
instruction.

**Verdict: `vice_recycle` proven live, deliberately, with the before-epoch, the recycle call, the
restarted signal, and the successful next call all recorded and dated.**
