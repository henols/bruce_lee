<objective>
Make the disposition ledger honest about what the shipped broker does, file the two review warnings
this gap closure deliberately did not fix, and measure the suite rather than compute it.

Purpose: criterion A's entire value is that the ledger is a truthful account of what changed, not a
row-count match. One row claimed a behaviour — a recycle whose supervisor respawns on the same port —
was re-observed, citing an acknowledgement test that only kills and a supervision test that exercised
a function the shipped broker never called. The row was not merely optimistic; it was false of the
shipped broker, and it was reported as its own verification gap for that reason. Plans 12 and 13 made
the claim true and wrote a test that observes it in the wired system, so the row can now be corrected
with a citation that holds. The same honesty applies to what was left undone: two real warnings are
out of scope by decision, and a decision recorded nowhere durable is indistinguishable from an
oversight.

Output: one corrected ledger row with a verbatim citation and a dated correction note, a
re-reconciled tally, a measured suite total with its pre-existing discrepancy stated rather than
smoothed over, two self-contained pending-todo items, and an explicit disposition for the
informational finding and the one carried deferred item.

Decisions this plan implements: **D-19** — correctness is claimed against specification and the
unverified set is itemised and owned, which is why the ledger row must cite a test that observes the
behaviour in the shipped system rather than one that observes it in a module; and **D-21** — the
verification boundary is written into the existing validation artifact rather than into a new one,
which is why the correction, the tally recount and the measured suite arithmetic all land in that same
file. Criterion E's own carry-forward list remains Phase 01.6.2.1's deliverable and is not authored
here.
</objective>
