## Decisions Made

- **Control-plane wire format: `as-specified`, no amendments.** Token auth, `0.0.0.0` bind, port `19510` default, newline-delimited JSON. Recorded in `.planning/RE-FINDINGS.md` with two accepted residual risks (host ephemeral-range confidence: MEDIUM; token strength bounded by the `D-1.2-D` uid-parity precondition: MEDIUM) and the unix-domain-socket auth alternative logged as a structural dead end (unreachable from the container across the Docker bridge).
- **`broker.json`'s refuse-to-clobber rule narrowed to "live pid only."** The tracer's own rule also refused on the mere presence of a `heartbeat_at` field — correct for a write-once tracer, but every record this long-lived broker itself writes carries `heartbeat_at`, so keeping that rule would make a crashed or stopped broker permanently unable to restart. Documented in `vice-broker.mts`'s own comment.
- **`VICE_ARGS`, when set, is used verbatim instead of the auto-constructed `-mcpserver` flags.** Matches `resources/vice-supervisor.sh`'s own existing convention for that variable, and is what lets `broker-e2e.test.ts` and manual smoke-testing stub a harmless binary (`/bin/sleep`) in place of `x64sc` without special-casing test code inside the broker itself.

## Issues Encountered

- **Manual smoke-testing during development surfaced a real design question, resolved in favor of the plan's own spec.** An early smoke test that closed its TCP connection immediately after receiving a grant saw its spawned child killed almost instantly — initially read as a bug, until re-examined: the plan's own design states "connection close IS the release... including on the client's own SIGKILL." A follow-up precisely-timed smoke test (`process.kill(pid, 0)` at t=1000ms with the connection still open, then again at t=1500ms after closing it) confirmed the child stays alive for as long as the connection is held open and is killed within 500ms of close — exactly the intended behavior, not a bug. No code change resulted; this is recorded here because it is exactly the kind of "gave a wrong first impression" hazard `.planning/RE-FINDINGS.md`'s hazard category exists for, though on reflection it did not meet that file's bar for a genuine finding (the behavior matched the plan's own explicit specification once re-checked, rather than surfacing a project-level RE technique or hazard) — noted here in the SUMMARY instead.
- **A single flaky-looking test failure occurred once, across roughly ten total full-suite runs, and did not reproduce.** No specific test name was captured before the terminal output scrolled past it (the assertion diff showed `actual: false` / `expected: true`, consistent with a timing-sensitive poll in one of the new async broker tests). Ten subsequent runs (targeted at the four new/changed broker test files, and the full suite) were all clean (0 failures). Given the inability to reproduce or isolate it, this is recorded as an observed anomaly rather than a fixed defect — if it recurs in a later plan's CI runs, the async poll helpers in `vice-broker-launch.test.ts`/`broker-e2e.test.ts` (`waitFor()`, `stopBroker()`) are the first place to look.
