<objective>
Wire per-instance crash supervision into the real broker entry point, and prove it against the
spawned broker artifact rather than against an injected stub.

Purpose: the supervision module is complete, exhaustively unit-tested, and never called by the
process that ships. Both real launch paths spawn through a bare spawn with no exit listener at all,
so a crashed or killed emulator child is never observed: its record survives forever with a dead
pid, its port can never be reallocated, and it permanently shrinks effective capacity. The phase
goal claims the broker owns per-instance supervision; today that sentence is false of the assembled
system while a fully green suite says otherwise. That gap between "the module behaves" and "the
system reaches the module" is the single hazard this whole gap closure exists to remove, so the
proof has to be taken at the real entry point.

Output: one exported supervision wrapper with exactly one exit-listener installation in the tree,
both real launch paths composed through it, a structural gate that fails if either path ever
regresses to an unwrapped spawn, and two end-to-end tests that kill a stub child out from under the
real spawned broker and watch it come back on the same port.

Decisions this plan implements: **D-04** — the per-instance epoch record's contract is unchanged and
a respawn advances its integer, which is the observable this plan's end-to-end proof reads; and
**D-23** — the per-instance boot and crash logs a human still reads keep their paths and their format
across a respawn, which is why no competing spawn factory is supplied on the supervision deps.
</objective>
