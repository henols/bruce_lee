structure, `broker.json` as the full discovery record carrying the control port and the capability
token, and the kernel-enforced singleton guard with its two distinct outcomes.

ROADMAP is explicit that the guard must be verified to hold rather than assumed.

and the singleton guard with both of its outcomes tested.

Close the blocker independently verified and carried since Phase 01.2: the retiring start path has
no singleton guard at all, so two concurrent brokers could race in a read-then-remove that is not
atomic across processes and hand the same ready instance to two sessions. A listener on a well-known
port cannot be bound twice, so this is kernel-enforced for the cost of binding it — but the ROADMAP
is explicit that it must be verified to hold rather than assumed.

Ordering, which is the substance of this task: evaluate the container guard, run the unconditional
reap, then BIND, and only then write the discovery record and begin accepting. Binding before writing
is what makes the kernel the singleton authority and reduces the record to an arbiter of which port
the broker's is. It also means a losing second broker can never overwrite the winner's record, which
is precisely the failure the old refuse-to-clobber heuristic was approximating.

On a bind failure with an address-in-use cause, consult the discovery record through the existing
container-side liveness classifier and take exactly one of two paths. A record classified alive means
this process is a second instance and the guard is working as designed: exit with status 0 and a
quiet message saying so. A record classified stale or never-started means the port is held by
something that is not a broker: exit non-zero with a loud message naming the port, naming that
nothing there answers as a broker, and naming what to check. Conflating these two would let a
squatted port masquerade as a healthy singleton, permanently and silently — which is why they are two
messages and not one. Any other bind failure cause is its own loud failure, not folded into either
path.

Record in the module's own comment, in prose a later reader will meet: the singleton guarantee holds
only while the control port keeps its default. Two brokers deliberately configured onto different
ports are two brokers and no code prevents that. State it rather than implying the guard is
unconditional.

first broker with the control port requested as zero, read the port it actually bound from its
discovery record, then start a second broker with that exact port configured. Assert the quiet

listener that is not a broker, start a third broker against it, and assert the loud path's non-zero
exit and its message. Assert in both cases that the discovery record on disk is byte-unchanged and
that no instance was launched.
