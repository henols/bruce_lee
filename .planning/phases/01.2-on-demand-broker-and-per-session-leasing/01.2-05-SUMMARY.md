## Decisions Made

- **D-1.2-C confirmed and written down**: the reset/clear-checkpoints/reload ritual is NARROWED, not retired. Fresh boot removes cross-*session* contamination structurally (a plan no longer needs the ritual to protect against a *previous session's* state), but does not remove contamination within a session that reuses one emulator across several plans — such a plan still opens with the ritual. This is the exact answer criterion 12 required, backed by task 1's evidence rather than declared by fiat.
- **Deliberate deviation from `workflow.human_verify_mode: end-of-phase`, honored end to end**: Task 1 was authored as a blocking `checkpoint:human-verify` (`gate="blocking-human"`) rather than an end-of-phase look, because criterion 13 is a sequencing gate — it needs a host-side broker process and two independent fresh Claude Code sessions, neither producible by an executor, and the ROADMAP/SKILL.md edit in Task 2 asserts a verdict the checkpoint is what establishes. The `blocking-human` token held throughout this execution: the checkpoint was presented to the human operator and explicitly approved (`resume-signal: "approved"`) before this continuation agent touched Task 2 — it was never auto-approved under any auto-mode path.
- **Three findings/deviations from Task 1's evidence, carried forward without overstatement** (per the resume instructions, none of these are invented beyond what the evidence document itself records):
  1. Task 1's `$FFFC` read (item 4) was made from the orchestrating session rather than a genuinely fresh one, at the operator's explicit direction — recorded as a deviation in the evidence doc's § 4. The gap this left (whether the `.mcp.json` `timeout` field re-triggers project-scope approval) was recovered independently in § 6 by session 2, a genuinely fresh session, which saw no approval prompt.
  2. The warm-spare refill is **grant-triggered, not release-triggered** (§ 7) — a deviation of the *system* from the procedure's expectation (which anticipated a post-release refill line), recorded plainly rather than smoothed over. This is plan 04's design working as built (refilling behind the grant hides cold-start latency), not a missed step.
  3. **Output-ceiling correction worth carrying forward**: a 16,384-byte `vice_memory_read` (~82,000 chars) arrived **inline** in one response (§ 8) — above the project's spike-findings 40–60 KB inline ceiling and below the proxy's declared 500,000-char ceiling. This settles the recorded discrepancy in this direction only: the standing 32 KB chunking rule is more conservative than the transport requires, and the 40–60 KB spike figure either is route-dependent (a different measurement path than the broker-granted proxy) or sits below the true limit. A single ~82 KB observation establishes only that the limit is *above* 82 KB on this route — the real boundary was not bisected, and this plan does not claim to have found it.
- **`install-resources.mjs` reused the existing `hostPath()`-with-`SET_ENV_HINT`-fallback shape verbatim** for the new broker path block rather than introducing new logic, and added no new import — the hostpath.mjs consumer set stays exactly the four traced production modules (`install-resources.mjs`, `vice-sync.mjs`, `ram-capture.mjs`, `vice-proxy.mjs`) plus `containerpath.mjs`'s sibling-import shape from the prior quick task; nothing new joins that closed set.
