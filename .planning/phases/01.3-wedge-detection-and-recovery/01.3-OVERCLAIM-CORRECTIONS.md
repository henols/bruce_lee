# Phase 01.3 — Overclaim Corrections

An adversarial audit of Phase 01.3's records found six overclaims — claims exceeding what their
evidence establishes — plus three items that were weak, not overclaiming, and needed softer wording.
This file records the audit's six findings and what each correction now says, following this
project's own convention: **correct, do not delete.** The wrong statement stays visible with its
reason, because the wrong turn is often the more instructive record. `01.3-TRIGGER-HUNT.md` and
`01.3-05-SUMMARY-attempt1-blocked.md` were audited and found clean; neither was touched. Only
`01.3-RECORDED-ANSWERS.md` and `COVERAGE.md` were edited. No ROADMAP.md criterion text was reworded —
the criteria are the fixed bar; only the records answering them moved.

Suite confirmed green during this correction pass, unrelated to the doc-only changes made:
**450 tests / 445 pass / 0 fail / 5 todo**, `node --test .claude/mcp/vice/*.test.ts` from the
worktree root.

## OC-1 — "0 of 6" rendered as six failed attempts

**Where:** `01.3-RECORDED-ANSWERS.md`, two locations — the "What therefore remains unproven"
paragraph under Criterion 11, and the "Neither half has met a real wedge" bullet under "What remains
unproven (D-07)."

**What was claimed:** the wedge was "sought on six separate deliberate attempts... without
reproducing" / "actively sought on six further bounded attempts... without reproducing once."

**What the evidence shows:** `01.3-TRIGGER-HUNT.md`'s own verdict is unambiguous — *"Denominator: 0 of
6 attempts... The budget is entirely unspent"* — with an attempt log that reads *"(Empty. Zero attempts
were made...)."* Zero attempts were made, not six unsuccessful ones. The hunt was blocked before
Variant A's first attempt when `vice_diagnose`/`vice_recycle` proved unreachable from that session's
own tool surface.

**Why the error likely happened:** `01.3-06-PLAN.md:279`, written 2026-08-02 *before* the hunt ran,
asserted "the trigger hunt ran to its stated six-attempt budget" — a plan-time expectation that
`01.3-RECORDED-ANSWERS.md` appears to have reproduced instead of checking against the hunt's own
recorded outcome. The same document states the correct 0-of-6 figure elsewhere (Criterion 9's
section, and Criterion 11's outcome line before this pass), so it contradicted itself.

**Correction now reads:** both locations now state "0 of 6 attempts... the budget is entirely
unspent" with an explicit note that an earlier draft overstated this as six attempts, and name the
likely origin (`01.3-06-PLAN.md:279`) as the probable source of the stale premise.

## OC-2 — "could not be produced on demand" overstates "never attempted"

**Where:** `01.3-RECORDED-ANSWERS.md`, Criterion 11's outcome line and the paragraph following the two
incident-record citations.

**What was claimed:** the outcome line read "Could not be produced on demand," and the following
paragraph reasoned that re-attempting the trigger hunt "would duplicate that plan's bounded-budget
discipline rather than answer this criterion with new evidence."

**What the evidence shows:** the only obstacle was the 2026-08-02 session's `vice_diagnose`/
`vice_recycle` unreachability, a session-specific gap that cleared 2026-08-04 (Phase 01.4). The
record's own Criterion 3 section makes live `vice_diagnose` calls in this very session, proving the
blocker no longer applies. `01.3-TRIGGER-HUNT.md`'s own resume condition ("re-attempt once
`vice_diagnose` and `vice_recycle` are confirmed reachable") is satisfied as of this writing. The
"would duplicate... discipline" reasoning was untrue — a first attempt is the only new evidence
available for a hunt that made zero attempts.

**Orchestrator decision recorded in the correction, verbatim per instruction:** the hunt is
deliberately **not** being run now. The developer has no machine access during this session, so if a
reproduction attempt wedges the emulator in a way `vice_recycle` cannot recover, nobody can restore
it. That downside is asymmetric against the upside of reproducing a state this phase can already
*detect* and *recover from*. Criterion 11's escape hatch rests on this choice — available and not
taken — which is a different, more honest state than "not producible."

**Correction now reads:** "NOT MET. Never attempted, not 'could not be produced on demand'" with the
narrower blocker, its 2026-08-04 clearing, and the orchestrator's asymmetric-risk decision recorded in
full.

## OC-3 — Criterion 10's stale mechanism and unfilled placeholder

**Where:** `01.3-RECORDED-ANSWERS.md`'s Criterion 10 section, and the parallel "guard, current shape"
section in `COVERAGE.md`.

**What was claimed:** a literal `PLACEHOLDER-PENDING-SUITE-RESULT` where a test count belonged; a
"four-layer guard" naming `handleToolsList()`/`handleToolsCall()` as an independent fourth layer and
describing a discovery-time filter as re-applying `DENY_LIST` "to whatever the manifest contains";
and a claim that dispatch of `vice_diagnose`/`vice_recycle` happens "before the `DENY_LIST` branch is
ever reached."

**What the evidence shows** (checked directly against `.claude/mcp/vice/vice-proxy.ts` and `vice.ts`
during this correction pass):

- `handleToolsList()` and `handleToolsCall()` no longer exist. Phase 01.6.3 replaced them:
  `tools/list` is answered entirely by `@mastra/mcp`'s own unmodified `ListToolsRequestSchema`
  handler; `tools/call` by a `CallToolRequestSchema` override.
- The described "layer 2" is `vice.ts`'s `serverInfo()`, which filters the **host's live `tools/list`
  RPC response**, not the manifest file. `refresh-manifest.ts` calls this same function to build the
  manifest — one mechanism, not two independent layers.
- The read-time filter was deliberately removed in favour of a construction-time skip
  (`if (DENY_LIST.includes(def.name)) continue;` when the in-memory `tools` object is built at
  startup).
- `tools-manifest.json` (63 entries, confirmed by direct read) contains `tools_list`, `tools_call`,
  `initialize` and `notifications_initialized` — four of the five `DENY_LIST` names are **present**,
  not absent, in the committed manifest. Only `vice_disk_list` is genuinely absent from the file.
  The safety property still holds regardless (construction-time filtering applies independent of the
  manifest's own contents), but "layer 1 covers all five names" is false for four of them.
- The dispatch-ordering claim was reversed: the `DENY_LIST` check is the literal first statement of
  the `CallToolRequestSchema` override, before any tool lookup — `vice_diagnose`/`vice_recycle` reach
  their dispatch by passing through that check, not by preceding it.
- The suite run during this pass: **450 tests / 445 pass / 0 fail / 5 todo** on `main`.

**Correction now reads:** the placeholder is replaced with the real count; the mechanism description
is rewritten to name the two layers that actually exist today (call-time refusal, construction-time
exclusion) plus the one property specific to `vice_disk_list` (absent from the manifest file itself);
the dispatch-ordering claim is corrected. The safety conclusion is **unchanged** — the guard still
holds — only the claimed mechanism was wrong. The identical stale description in `COVERAGE.md`'s "The
guard 01.1's OPT-OUT row rests on, current shape" section was corrected the same way, since it feeds
this phase's own criterion 10 directly and would otherwise contradict the freshly-corrected
`RECORDED-ANSWERS.md` section right next to it.

## OC-6 — criterion 8 claims a hard-rule prohibition where a discretionary decision exists

**Where:** `01.3-RECORDED-ANSWERS.md`, Criterion 8 section, point 2 ("The boundary, and why") and the
paragraph beginning "The delivered container-side detection plus unconditional recycle is exactly the
fallback..."

**What was claimed:** that host-process introspection "is out of scope by the project's own hard rule"
(`.claude/CLAUDE.md`'s `mcp__vice__*`-only-route rule), and that "the criterion is answered by the
shape of the deliverable, not evaded by it."

**What the evidence shows:** the hard rule forbids a **second route** to the emulator — a script or
module opening its own connection, reading broker state, importing a transport module. It does not
forbid this project's own host-side broker (which already spawns `x64sc` as its child, records its
pid, and writes heartbeats) from observing that child host-side. The boundary was closed by
**decision** — D-12 (`01.3-CONTEXT.md:113`): *"Criterion 8 is discharged by decision, and the phase
says so."* Separately, criterion 8's own text is conditional ("**If** the host cannot distinguish a
wedged instance from a busy one..."); nothing was ever investigated to establish that antecedent
either way, so "the criterion is answered" assumes what was never shown.

**Correction now reads:** point 2 now attributes the boundary to D-11/D-12's decision rather than the
hard rule, and explains why the rule does not categorically forbid host-side observation of the
broker's own child. The following paragraph is reworded to say the deliverable *matches* the
criterion's own named fallback, without claiming the antecedent was established — the outcome line
("DISCHARGED BY DECISION, not by evidence") and point 1 ("Not investigated") were already correct and
are unchanged.
